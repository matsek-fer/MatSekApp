## Pitch

Before learning a field it helps to know what fields there are, how they talk
to each other, and what kind of thinking each one rewards. The materials here
are not courses; they are atlases. Several of them are referenced from
individual topics ("see the Napkin chapter on …"), so they are listed once
here.

## Materials

### Evan Chen — An Infinitely Large Napkin
- **Link:** https://web.evanchen.cc/napkin.html
- **Type:** notes
- **Difficulty:** 2-4
- **Free:** yes
- **Strengths:** One PDF (~1000 pages) that sketches essentially every
  undergraduate and early-graduate field — algebra, topology, analysis,
  measure theory, category theory, algebraic geometry, representation theory
  — in short chapters that explain what each subject is *about* and why its
  main theorems matter. Written for someone who wants the big picture fast.
- **Weaknesses:** Deliberately a sketch: proofs are abbreviated, exercises are
  few. You learn what to learn, not the thing itself.

### Evan Chen — website
- **Link:** https://web.evanchen.cc/
- **Type:** site
- **Difficulty:** 1-4
- **Free:** yes
- **Strengths:** Home of the Napkin, dozens of olympiad handouts, course
  notes from MIT/Harvard classes, and short essays on how to learn and do
  mathematics. The "advice" pages are worth reading before anything else.
- **Weaknesses:** Olympiad-centric; the undergraduate notes are uneven in
  polish.

### Real Not Complex
- **Link:** https://realnotcomplex.com/
- **Type:** site
- **Difficulty:** 1-5
- **Free:** yes
- **Strengths:** A hand-curated index of legally free textbooks and lecture
  notes, one page per field, with short reviews. The first place to look for
  a free alternative to any paid book listed in these topic files.
- **Weaknesses:** An index, not a path; the reviews are brief and the quality
  of the indexed material varies.

### Eugenia Cheng — The Joy of Abstraction
- **Link:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/joy-of-abstraction/00D9AFD3046A406CB85D1AFF5450E657
- **Type:** book
- **Difficulty:** 1-2
- **Free:** no
- **Strengths:** Category theory explained from zero to a reader who has
  never written a proof, by someone who thinks hard about *why* abstraction
  is worth it. The first half is about mathematical thinking in general and
  works as a gentle pitch for the whole of mathematics.
- **Weaknesses:** Slow and chatty for anyone with a maths background; the
  category theory it reaches is introductory.

### Timothy Gowers (ed.) — The Princeton Companion to Mathematics
- **Link:** https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691118802/the-princeton-companion-to-mathematics
- **Type:** book
- **Difficulty:** 2-4
- **Free:** no
- **Strengths:** ~1000 pages of essays by leading mathematicians: the
  branches of mathematics, ~100 fundamental concepts, ~30 major theorems and
  problems, biographies, and "the influence of mathematics". The best answer
  to "what is X and why does anyone care" for almost any X.
- **Weaknesses:** Expensive and heavy; essays vary in difficulty with no
  signposting. Reference, not a read-through.

### Saunders Mac Lane — Mathematics: Form and Function
- **Link:** https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4612-4872-9
- **Type:** book
- **Difficulty:** 3-4
- **Free:** no
- **Strengths:** A co-founder of category theory surveys all of mathematics
  — number, geometry, calculus, algebra, mechanics, logic — and asks what the
  subject actually is and how its parts fit. The closest thing to a
  philosophy of mathematics written by someone who built a lot of it.
- **Weaknesses:** Assumes you already know the mathematics being surveyed;
  dated in places (1986); dense.

## YouTube channels

### 3Blue1Brown
- **Link:** https://www.youtube.com/@3blue1brown
- **Type:** video
- **Difficulty:** 1-2
- **Free:** yes
- **Strengths:** The gold standard for visual intuition. The *Essence of
  Calculus* and *Essence of Linear Algebra* series are the recommended
  starting point for those topics.
- **Weaknesses:** Intuition only — no proofs, no exercises.

### Michael Penn
- **Link:** https://www.youtube.com/@MichaelPennMath
- **Type:** video
- **Difficulty:** 2-3
- **Free:** yes
- **Strengths:** Near-daily problem-solving videos at proof level plus full
  lecture-course playlists (real analysis, abstract algebra, number theory,
  differential equations). Watching him *solve* is the most useful part.
- **Weaknesses:** Blackboard-and-marker; the course playlists follow a
  standard syllabus without much motivation.

### Mathemaniac
- **Link:** https://www.youtube.com/@mathemaniac
- **Type:** video
- **Difficulty:** 1-3
- **Free:** yes
- **Strengths:** Long-form explainers with a geometric bent — group theory,
  complex analysis, Lie groups, the Erlangen program — that go further than
  most pop-maths channels.
- **Weaknesses:** Irregular uploads; some videos assume more than they say.

### Sheafification of G
- **Link:** https://www.youtube.com/@SheafificationOfG
- **Type:** video
- **Difficulty:** 3-4
- **Free:** yes
- **Strengths:** Category theory, sheaves, type theory and homotopy type
  theory with real definitions and real proofs, delivered with humour. One
  of very few channels at this level.
- **Weaknesses:** Fast; not for a first contact with the subjects.
